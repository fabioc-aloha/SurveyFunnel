Master Plan of Record

QUICK CHALLENGE

Six questions, 48 credits, one launch. The audience, list preparation, and Honest Ask invitation are provided. Any question can be cut, but at least one must remain. Three questions are the BRD cards: keep and repair all three to win. Cutting one leaves that requirement unmet. Three more arrived unrequested: repair or cut each one, because an unresolved extra leaves the challenge unfinished.

Sending costs 30 credits, the invitation costs 4, and the three BRD repairs cost 6 in total. You also need response above 12%, quality 60+, and Insight 60+. Each run also draws one BRD twist from the run seed: a stakeholder demand (a named extra that must stay in the instrument and be repaired), a raised bar (a higher response threshold or a 95+ quality gate), or a time ceiling on the repaired instrument, read from its estimated minutes. The BRD names the twist and its acceptance line. The challenge banner repeats the acceptance line, and the report rules on whether the twist was met.

Most runs also draw a complication after the first report (a staler list than the brief stated, a question added by a stakeholder, or a sponsor demanding a second push): accept it and a follow-up wave is set up on the credits left in the same 48, or decline it and the first result stands. The final verdict then reads both waves together.

Retry resets this challenge and draws a new brief. Next Challenge changes the six cards. Neither changes campaign progress.

RESPONDENT REACTIONS

Speech bubbles illustrate events in the simulation, such as guessing, a DK/NA escape, or encountering a defective question. They are not real customer quotes and do not change scores. Only a few bubbles appear at a readable pace. The report keeps a short selection, even if you skip the animation.

STAKEHOLDER ENCOUNTERS

In Levels 3 and 6, a stakeholder asks for eight extra questions. Accept the request, negotiate rotating modules, or show the evidence for keeping the survey focused. Read the projected minutes, goodwill, and credit costs before choosing; you may change your choice before launch. The extras are modeled as added burden, not separately editable cards or new scored agenda topics. Showing evidence is the default if no choice is made. The report ties the conversation to the run's actual results.

HOW TO PLAY

  • Choose a level, then set the audience, invitation, and questionnaire within the credit budget.
  • Launch the survey, watch the funnel, and review the response, data quality, and Insight Value scores.
  • Change one choice and replay to see how it affects the results.
  • Campaign mode tracks six levels; winning a level unlocks the next. New Game resets campaign progress and deals a new questionnaire.

CONTROLS

  • Use the Sound panel to adjust music and effects, preview audio, and turn hints on or off. The sound button at the top toggles audio; press M to mute.
  • During a run, choose 1× or 2× speed, or skip ahead to the report.
  • Browsers start audio after your first click. Preferences save on this device.

SIMULATION AND PRIVACY

All outcomes are simulated from your choices. The game sends no email and uses no real customer data. The same instrument and choices produce identical results from the same starting state. A retry changes that state: credits, goodwill, reputation, and owned preparation carry over.

THE LIST: BRONZE, SILVER, GOLD

  • Bronze / Silver / Gold layers: the list pipeline. Bronze holds records exactly as they arrive: duplicates, broken addresses, stale records and all. Silver is where records are cleaned: duplicates merged, addresses repaired, the unfixable quarantined. Gold is the promoted contact master: only records that survived Silver are mailed from it. Each layer's output is the next layer's input, so a defect skipped in Silver is mailed from Gold.
  • Dedupe vs consolidation: dedupe merges records the same system created twice (same address, two rows). Consolidation merges records that arrived from different sources about the same person, where the addresses differ. Neither sees the other's duplicates; a list can need both.
  • Repair vs validation: repair fixes an address before promotion (a known typo pattern, a moved office). Validation checks deliverability at send time and removes what would bounce. Repair works on what the list knows; validation tests what the mail system will accept. They are not substitutes.
  • Quarantine: holding a record out of promotion without deleting it. An unfixable address is quarantined in Silver so it never reaches Gold; the record stays auditable instead of silently vanishing.
  • Derived list health: the invalid rate is computed from what the list actually contains and what work was done on it: stale records plus unrepaired broken addresses, divided by the records promoted. It is not a fixed property of a list; the same list is healthier after Silver work and worse after none.

THE THREE SCORES

  • Response rate: completed questionnaires divided by invitations actually sent, including bounced invitations. This is the game's simplified measure, not an AAPOR response rate: eligibility and full case dispositions are not modeled. A higher rate does not establish lower nonresponse bias.
  • Data quality: a 0-100 game index of simulated measurement risks. Guesses, speeding, straightlining, and missing core items lower it. It is not a percentage of valid answers.
  • Insight Value: did the business topics this survey exists for (the agenda) come back answered, by the people those topics are about? A fast survey that answers nothing scores zero here, and zero here loses the level.

Data quality and Insight Value are teaching-game indices, not validated research metrics. Insight Value represents agenda coverage and respondent-group fit, not proof of construct validity or population representativeness.

GOODWILL AND REPUTATION

  • Goodwill: how much patience a list has left for you. Long surveys, reminders, and questions people cannot answer spend it. At zero, the breaker stops new sends and ends the run for anyone still in the funnel. Only completions before the trip count.
  • Sender reputation: whether mail systems trust your domain. Dead addresses (hard bounces) damage it; damaged reputation means spam folders eat your opens.
  • DK/NA escapes: "Don't know / Not applicable" options let respondents continue with item nonresponse instead of a forced answer. They do not make the remaining answers valid or remove the burden of an irrelevant question.
  • Satisficing: what tired respondents do instead of thinking: rushing, picking the same answer down a grid, parking in the middle. Length and burden cause it.

THE 11 DEFECT CLASSES AND THEIR IN-GAME REPAIRS

Each class has one repair button in this game. In practice, revisions, routing, response options, and pretesting depend on the construct and target population. A privacy statement must describe protections that actually exist.

  • DOUBLE-BARRELED (two questions in one item): fix is SPLIT.
  • LEADING (wording pushes a happy answer): fix is NEUTRALIZE.
  • JARGON (insider words the audience does not share): fix is DEFINE.
  • VAGUE TIMEFRAME ("recently", "usually", no period named): fix is ANCHOR.
  • RECALL OVERLOAD (asking for counts over a year): fix is SHORTEN WINDOW.
  • BEYOND KNOWLEDGE (this audience cannot know it): no wording fix. CUT it, or buy DK/NA escapes.
  • DOUBLE NEGATIVE (negatives stacked until it flips meaning): fix is REWORD.
  • BROKEN OPTIONS (answer ranges overlap or miss the truth): fix is FIX OPTIONS.
  • GRID BURDEN (a big rating matrix, worst on phones): fix is BREAK UP.
  • SENSITIVE, NO ASSURANCE (a risky question with no privacy promise): fix is ADD PRIVACY LINE.
  • WORDY STEM (a paragraph where a sentence would do): fix is SIMPLIFY. It is the only class Simplify fixes.

QUESTION VALUE AND BURDEN

  • LOW-VALUE (TURN-OFF RISK): vanity, blame, irrelevant personal questions, trivia, and duplicates contribute no agenda insight. Some also contain leading wording or unsupported assumptions. In this game they earn zero insight, cost goodwill, and can only be CUT.
  • FILLER (LOW VALUE): harmless but empty. Full time cost, half insight credit at best. Cut when the agenda needs the room.

WIN GATES

In the campaign, produce evidence stakeholders can trust and use. Pass all five checks: funnel execution, evidence fitness, data integrity, audience sustainability, and operational trust. Response rate is a benchmark, not a win gate. Only cleaned, account-linked responses count toward the mission's sample and role requirements; Insight is calculated again from those responses. Quality must remain 60+ and Insight 60+ (Level 6: 70+). The BRD states the mission's minimum sample and required roles. These are simulation rules, not statistical guarantees.

After FINISH the report follows CLEAN → MATCH → INTERPRET → PUBLISH → ACT. Speeders, straight-liners, duplicate, and junk responses are removed once each; forwarded responses cannot be matched to the invited account. Guessed responses stay in the data, because real cleaning cannot detect them; the report counts the guesses it can see and prices them in Quality and Insight. INTERPRET answers the BRD's decision questions with simulated findings read off the usable base; a topic that did not answer credibly reads CANNOT SAY there, and no number is invented for it. A failed check withholds publication and names the next action. The report's Download scorecard button saves the publishable artifact (mission, checks, BRD acceptance, dispositions, context note) as a Markdown file. Declaring an A/B winner on a gap inside the uncertainty range goes on the campaign record: the next stakeholder debrief and the season grade name it. Audience health carries across instruments in this campaign. Starting a new instrument does not refresh the audience; resetting the whole campaign does. Quick Challenges retain their own response, quality, Insight, and BRD requirements.

FISHBOWL

How a survey list is built, from raw records to the responses that come back. Step 2 of this game compresses the first stages into one step; this is the whole flow it stands for.

SOURCES

Contact records arrive from several internal systems, including CRM. Prior survey responses are a source too. The same person can exist in several systems at once, under the same address or different ones, and one person can belong to several surveys.

BRONZE

Records land exactly as they arrive. No cleanup and no validation happen at this layer: what arrives is what is kept, duplicates, typos, and stale addresses included.

SILVER

Silver is the cleaning layer, and three kinds of work happen here. Dedupe merges rows one system created twice: same system, same address, two rows. Consolidation merges records that arrived from different sources about the same person, where the addresses differ. Syntax repair fixes the address typos that can be fixed. An address that cannot be repaired is flagged and is not promoted to Gold.

GOLD · CONTACT MASTER

Gold is the consolidated contact master table: one record per email address, refreshed daily, carrying the enhancements gathered from the other sources, including historical survey results. Every send is built from this table.

In the game: Step 2's three panels are this pipeline compressed into one step. The game skips the daily refresh and deals you the intake for the audience you chose.

SAMPLING

Sampling runs in a separate environment. It verifies a larger set of addresses than the sample needs, in bulk, then prepares the sample from the verified set, stratified by region and customer segment. Most addresses flagged "risky" are sent anyway; only invalid addresses are excluded. Excluded invalids and hard bounces are kept in tables, so they are not mailed again.

In the game: the validation choice in Step 2's Gold panel is this check, simplified to two tiers.

PERMISSION

Before the final sample is locked, a permission service is checked for every contact. It rules on opt-outs and returns a personalized unsubscribe link to put in the invite. The link is passed to the sending platform and is not stored. The send has to go out within 72 hours of the check; after that, the check no longer holds.

In the game: this is the suppression scrub in Step 2, without the 72-hour clock.

THE SEND

The prepared sample is loaded to the survey platform by API. Sends are organized by recipient time zone, so the invite lands near the top of the inbox in the morning.

RESPONSES AND DISPOSITION

Responses and disposition data are pulled back daily by API into the data platform. When a wave ends, the incomplete responses are flushed across as well. Waves run monthly. Results flow out to the departments that analyze them; disposition data (who was sent to, who bounced, who opted out) stays inside the survey team.

In the game: your round report is one wave, compressed. Its deliverability section is the disposition view.

WHY IT MATTERS

List quality is decided between Bronze and Gold, long before anyone presses send. Most of the survey problems an Instrument Manager meets at send time (bounces, duplicates, people who should never have been mailed) were baked in upstream. That is why Step 2 exists.
