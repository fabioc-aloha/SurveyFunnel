# Dear Valued Customer - Player Guide

You are an Instrument Manager. Your job: produce evidence stakeholders
can trust and use, while protecting the audience. A completed questionnaire
is only the start: the evidence must survive cleaning, account matching,
sample and role checks, and interpretation. One round is one simulated
survey launch, 3 to 5 minutes.

## Quick Challenge

Choose **Quick Challenge** for six cards and one launch: three BRD
cards with defects, plus three questions the BRD never asked for.
The Fresh audience, full list preparation, and Honest Ask invitation
are provided. Repair all three BRD cards and resolve each extra,
by repair or by cut, within 48 credits, then reach response above
12%, quality 60+, and Insight 60+. Any question can be cut, but at least
one must remain. Cutting a BRD card leaves its requirement unmet;
keeping an unresolved extra also leaves the challenge unfinished.

Each run also draws one **BRD twist** from the run seed, so the same
six cards stop having one memorized answer. A twist is one of three
kinds: a stakeholder demand (a named extra becomes required: it
must be kept and repaired to satisfy the twist), a raised bar (a
higher response threshold, or a quality gate of 95+ instead of 60),
or a time ceiling on the repaired instrument, read from its
estimated minutes. The challenge banner and the BRD name the twist
and its acceptance line; the report rules on whether it was met.
The seed is printed in the BRD: the same seed always deals the same
brief, and a retry draws a new one.

Most runs also draw a **complication** after the first report: the
list was older than the brief stated (the real invalid rate is 9%,
and the provided validation does not carry to a second wave), a
stakeholder adds one required question (repair it or carry it as written
to satisfy the request; cutting it leaves the challenge unfinished), or the sponsor demands a second push
to the people who never opened (reminder waves at the usual price).
Accepting sets up a **second wave** on the credits left in the same
48; the pool never refills, and only wave 2's own actions are
charged. Declining is free and makes the wave 1 result final. If
you accept, the final verdict reads both waves together:
completions and sends are summed for the response rate, quality
and Insight are read from the final instrument's wave, and the BRD
repairs, extras, and twist are re-checked against the instrument
you end with. A lost wave 1 can be rescued, and a won one can be
put at risk, by the choices the complication forces.

The send costs 30 credits, the invitation costs 4, and the three
BRD repairs cost 6. **Retry Challenge** starts fresh with the same cards;
**Next Challenge** switches scenarios. Neither changes campaign
progress or spends the campaign's credits.

## A campaign round, step by step

1. **AUDIENCE.** Pick a segment. Read its size, persona mix,
   relevance, contact history, the intake mix its list arrives with
   (duplicates, broken and stale records), and starting goodwill
   before you commit.
2. **Build the list.** The list arrives in Bronze, raw. Buy Silver
   work with credits: dedupe (merges same-system duplicates),
   consolidate (merges second-source duplicates, one record per
   person), repair addresses (fixes fixable addresses, quarantines
   the unfixable). What survives is promoted to the Gold contact
   master; its list health is derived from what arrived and what you
   fixed, and stale records have no fix. Then the Gold checks: email
   validation (BriteVerify) at skip, partial, or full; suppression
   scrub (opt outs and recently surveyed); localization review.
   These are simulated preparation choices, not live service integrations.
3. **INVITATION.** Pick a variant and a reminder count. Generic is
   free and weak. Honest Ask states a time promise you choose
   (3, 5, 8, or 10 minutes) and explains why the recipient was
   selected; keep the questionnaire within the printed promise.
   Known Name opens best and costs coordination; it earns its
   goodwill only once the audience pool has heard from you before,
   and on a first contact its subject makes no claim about a fix.
4. **QUESTIONNAIRE.** The fixed starting instrument has 12 questions;
   New Game deals 9 to 16. Keep, apply the card's class-specific repair,
   or cut each question. Watch the estimated
   minutes against each persona's patience. Add escapes (Don't know
   / Not applicable) to allow item nonresponse instead of forced answers.
5. **LAUNCH.** Every recipient in the selected audience is simulated.
   Animated sprites represent groups with similar behavior, each
   labeled `×N`. There may be more than 40 groups. Counters show
   individual simulated recipients, not the number of visible sprites.
   Groups run through SENT, DELIVERED, OPENED, STARTED, FINISH. A breaker trip stops
   new sends and interrupts anyone still in the funnel. Only
   completions before the trip count. Respondent bubbles illustrate
   actual simulated events; they add no penalties or randomness.
   The report retains a few reactions even when you skip the animation.
6. **REPORT.** The Drop-off Detective shows the funnel, the three
   biggest leaks with causes and fixing levers, the goodwill and
   credit ledgers, deliverability and data quality, and one fix
   quest. Then TRY AGAIN if credits permit, open LEVEL SELECT, or
   choose NEXT LEVEL after a win. Level 6 offers SEASON RESULTS.
   The Evidence Fitness Scorecard checks all five dimensions independently.
   FINISH continues through CLEAN, MATCH, INTERPRET, PUBLISH, and ACT.
   Failed checks withhold publication and name the next actions; passing
   results include a context note with the response base and limitations.

## The two budgets

**Goodwill (0 to 100 per audience pool).** Questions beyond a lean core,
reminders, and unanswerable questions spend it. Short surveys, an
honest time ask, a why me line, and closing the loop earn it back.
At zero the breaker stops new invitations and ends the run for anyone
still in the funnel. Only completions before the trip count.
Fresh, Stale, and Tired share the Support pool; Global and Executive Blast
have separate pools. Starting another level or questionnaire retains pool
health and contact history. Additional campaign sends reduce starting
goodwill by one per previous send with suppression, or two without, capped
at eight; retries retain their four-point fatigue charge instead.
Lower health also reduces opens. Suppression is required when recontacting
a pool; it reduces fatigue but does not restore health. This is a cohort
model, not individual customer tracking. It resets on page reload or a
whole-campaign New Game, not on a new instrument.

**Credits (120 per level, 150 in Level 6, one pool across all your
attempts).**

An over-budget setup cannot launch; reduce costs before sending.

## After a round: Try Again or New Game

- **Try Again (continue).** Your level budget is one pool for the
  whole level, not a refill. You return to setup with the credits
  you have left, your prep purchases and your edited questionnaire
  intact, and goodwill where you left it. Each new send costs its
  send price again, new edits cost their action prices, and
  re-sending costs 4 goodwill: the audience remembers. Improve the
  process until an attempt passes all five evidence checks, or the money runs
  out. If the remaining budget cannot cover another send and the
  target is unmet, the level is failed.
  The minimum batch costs 30 credits, or 45 for Executive Blast.
  Cost-reducing choices remain available even when you need several
  reductions to make the inherited setup affordable. Owning preparation
  does not pay for a new batch, invitation, or reminders.
- **New Game.** A fresh start on the level with a new random
  questionnaire and a fresh credit pool: new questions and the level's
  default segment, but the campaign's audience history remains.
  The instrument's seed code identifies
  the draw; there is no player-facing seed-entry control. From the start screen, New
  Game starts a whole fresh campaign, as does New Game on a LEVEL FAILED
  report. Those whole-campaign resets clear audience history.

## Stakeholder encounters

In Levels 3 and 6, a stakeholder asks for eight more questions.
Choose **Show the Evidence** (no added cost), **Negotiate Modules**
(4 credits, +1 minute, -3 goodwill), or **Accept the Request**
(10 credits, +4 minutes, -10 goodwill).

Each choice shows the projected survey length, ending goodwill,
and total credit spend. Unaffordable choices are disabled. You can
change your response before launch; the report compares the chosen
trade-off with the run's completion and patience results.

The extras are modeled as added burden. They are not editable cards,
and extra-topic answers are not simulated or scored.

## Sound

The cabinet has a soundtrack: an original chiptune loop that
swells during a run, plus effects wired to what happens (spending,
scanning, bounces, finishes, the breaker). The SOUND panel on the
start screen has music and effects toggles, volumes, and preview
buttons; the speaker button in the header and the M key mute
everything. Your settings are remembered on the device. Audio
starts after your first click, as browsers require.

## Question defects and their fixes

Any question may be cut for 1 credit, including core, BRD-required,
and late-added questions. When only one remains, its CUT button is
gray and disabled. Restoring a cut question or adding a bank question
enables cutting again; an empty instrument cannot launch.

Permission to cut is separate from study acceptance. Missing core
items incur the report's 10-point-per-item quality deduction; losing
required agenda coverage lowers Insight. Quick Challenges cannot be
won with missing BRD cards, an unmet mandatory twist, or a cut required
late question. A shorter questionnaire can therefore perform better
in the funnel while producing a worse result.

Every defective question has one named defect, and the fix button
names its in-game repair. Real questionnaire revisions also depend on
the construct, routing, respondent population, and pretesting.
Simplifying does not fix everything:

- **Double-barreled** (two judgments, one answer): SPLIT it, or
  cut one half.
- **Leading** (wording pushes an answer): NEUTRALIZE it.
- **Jargon / acronym**: DEFINE it in plain words.
- **Vague timeframe** ("recently"): ANCHOR it to a real window.
- **Recall overload** (counting a year of events): SHORTEN the
  window.
- **Beyond knowledge** (they cannot know this): CUT it, or add
  Don't Know escapes.
- **Double negative**: REWORD it positive.
- **Broken options** (overlap, or no fit): FIX the options.
- **Grid burden** (long matrix): BREAK IT UP, or cut rows.
- **Sensitive, no assurance**: ADD a privacy line only if the stated
  protections actually exist.
- **Wordy stem**: SIMPLIFY it.

The Detective only blames a defect for damage it actually saw in
your run; the rest are flagged as risks. The full field guide is
`docs/question-defects.md`.

## Insight Value after cleaning

Each level brief lists an agenda: the business topics the survey
exists to answer. Insight Value (0-100) credits a topic only when
its question is in your final survey, with credit adjusted for observed
measurement risks, answerability, and the mix of completing respondent
groups. Low-value items
earn nothing and cost Insight and goodwill. In the campaign, Insight
is recomputed using only cleaned, account-linked responses. Excluded
respondents cannot satisfy persona coverage or earn agenda credit.
Insight must reach 60+ (70+ in the finale), alongside the other
evidence checks. A high-rate run with inadequate Insight can still
be stamped FAST AND USELESS.

## Learning the trade

The **? Consult MPOR** button opens the **Master Plan of Record**,
which explains the game rules and methodology in plain words.
Reports retain the Detective diagnostics and fix quests, without
a separate coaching banner or lesson recap. Setup steps are numbered
1 to 4, and the ready checklist above LAUNCH shows what you have
decided so far; it never blocks you.

**? BRD** in the top bar opens the **Business Requirement Document** for
the selected or active study from any screen. Setup repeats the link at
the top, in a banner naming that level's own BRD, and beside the BRD
required topics in the agenda panel. It lists required topics and
respondent groups, sample, role, quality, and Insight requirements,
credit budget, and level-specific constraints. Quick Challenge has its
own BRD. Unlike MPOR's shared rules, the BRD changes with the level;
consulting it does not change your choices.

## Deliverability, in 20 seconds

Invalid addresses bounce. Hard bounces damage Sender Reputation
(starts at 90). Over 5% hard bounces and opens fall as mail lands
in spam. Over 10% and the game raises a domain reputation warning.
Validation removes most invalid addresses before the send. A small
share always survives, because catch all domains cannot be
verified.

## Scoring and winning

- **Response rate:** completed questionnaires divided by invitations
  actually sent, including bounces. This simplified game measure is
  not an AAPOR response rate; eligibility and full case dispositions
  are not modeled. Campaign response benchmarks are diagnostic;
  Quick Challenges still enforce their specified response thresholds.
  Displayed percentages use up to three decimals; acceptance checks
  use the unrounded rate.
- **Data quality score:** starts at 100. Guesses, speeders, straight
  liners, duplicates, forwarded responses, and missing core
  questions subtract. Escapes used add a small bonus.
- **Insight Value:** agenda coverage adjusted for simulated measurement
  risks, answerability, and the completing respondent groups.
- **Campaign win:** pass every check below. Scores are never averaged
  to override a failed check. Winning grades S, A, and B use the
  weakest of quality, usable Insight, and cleaning retention, not rate.
  Non-winning grades remain C or TRY AGAIN.

| Check | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Funnel execution | At least one sent invitation and completion, without a breaker trip |
| Evidence fitness | Correct audience pool; mission minimum usable N; every required role; at least 80% of cleaned responses matched to the invited account |
| Data integrity | Quality 60+; at least 60% of completions survive cleaning; at least one retained response |
| Audience sustainability | Ending goodwill above zero (35+ in Level 4); suppression on repeat contact |
| Operational trust | List validation and dedupe; usable Insight 60+ (70+ in Level 6); localization review and localized invitation for Global |

| Mission | Required pool | Minimum usable simulated N | Required role coverage |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| First Send | Support | 750 | Technicians and end users, each at least 10% |
| The Split | Support | 1,000 | Technicians and end users, each at least 10% |
| The Stakeholder | Support | 1,000 | Technicians and end users, each at least 10% |
| The Tired List | Support | 675 | Technicians and end users, each at least 10% |
| Global Fieldwork | Global | 900 | Technicians and end users, each at least 10% |
| The Big Quarter | Leadership | 1,500 | Executives and IT decision makers together at least 50% |

Each required role group must contain at least one usable respondent.
The usable sample is the actual number of cleaned, account-linked
recipient records. Animation cohorts neither inflate this count nor
determine its size. These are programmed simulation outcomes, not
observations of real customers.
Cleaning excludes duplicate, junk, speeder, and straight-lined
responses, once each in that order. Guessed responses stay in the
usable sample: real cleaning cannot detect them. The report counts
the guesses it can see and prices them in Quality and Insight. Forwarded responses have no verified
invited-account association and are excluded at MATCH. The original quality
score still diagnoses fieldwork defects; cleaning does not erase their
penalties. Publication requires all five checks, and the ACT step names
the corrections needed or the decision the evidence can support.
Every required topic must come back answered credibly, Level 1's
list must sit inside its BRD's 30-day audience window, mailing an
opted-out or recently surveyed person fails operational trust
(the scrub removes those records before the send), and the report
closes with a line-by-line BRD acceptance and a final disposition
for every sent case, with AAPOR RR3 and RR4 beside the game rate.

Guessed-completion and duplicate deductions use their rates among
invited recipients, not raw population-sized counts. Their game weights
are 160 and 120 respectively, rounded to quality points. Other response
flags use shares of completions. Missing core questions still cost
10 quality points each; changing animation density changes no score.

Data quality and Insight Value are game indices, not validated research
metrics. Neither a high rate nor a winning score establishes construct
validity, population representativeness, or low nonresponse bias.

The same instrument and choices produce identical outcomes from the same
starting state. A retry changes credits, goodwill, reputation, and owned prep.

## Levels and difficulty

1. **First Send - Easy.** Learn audience selection, list preparation,
   invitation design, and questionnaire repair.
2. **The Split - Easy.** Optional randomized A/B invitation comparison.
   Interpret uncertainty; the verdict buttons do not change scores.
3. **The Stakeholder - Medium.** Mid build, a stakeholder demands 8 more
   questions. Accept, push back, or rotate modules.
4. **The Tired List - Hard.** A fatigued segment at low goodwill. Win it
   back with a short instrument and visible closing of the loop.
5. **Global Fieldwork - Medium.** Five simulated language groups.
   Review invitation language, localization, and language-specific nonresponse.
6. **The Big Quarter - Hard.** Executive Blast, stakeholder burden,
   and the higher Insight gate.

These labels describe scenario complexity, not automatic wins or separate
difficulty modes. The recorded corpus includes a legal winner for each
fixed start, plus the sampled seeded instruments listed in the
[development notes](development.md#initial-budget-winnability-and-difficulty).
The Tired List has a smaller recorded sample than the other levels.
These fixtures do not guarantee that every possible seed is winnable.

## Tips from the Detective

- Bounces are the cheapest problem to fix and the most expensive
  to ignore. Validate any list you did not build yourself.
- The reminder that recovers 140 completions at 5 goodwill and 8
  credits is a decision, not a default.
- If technicians quit at one question, the question is wrong for
  them, not the technicians.
- A high rate from the wrong people loses Insight Value. Tight
  targeting beats a heroic blast.
